# Study Screening Log

Screening date: 2026-07-12

## Review Question

How do the present Lahore study's findings on maternal education, menstrual-hygiene knowledge, and menstrual-hygiene practice compare with published studies of adolescent girls in Pakistan and comparable low- and middle-income settings?

## Search Process

PubMed and OpenAlex were searched with the following queries:

1. `Pakistan adolescent girls menstrual hygiene maternal education`
2. `Pakistan schoolgirls menstrual hygiene knowledge practice`
3. `South Asia adolescent menstrual hygiene maternal education practice`
4. `adolescent girls menstrual hygiene knowledge practice parental education`

The complete returned identifiers and records are retained in `search_results.json`. DOI metadata were checked independently through Crossref and the DOI Handle API. Google Search was also attempted but was unavailable because the configured search service returned HTTP 403; it was not counted as a completed source.

## Inclusion Criteria

- Primary research involving adolescent girls or a predominantly adolescent/young-woman population.
- Menstrual knowledge, hygiene practice, maternal/parental education, information source, or enabling-resource outcomes.
- Priority order: Pakistan, other South Asian settings, then one contextual study with directly comparable maternal-education models.
- Verifiable DOI and bibliographic identity.
- Full text available through a trusted repository or publisher, except one abstract-level study retained solely for its clearly reported knowledge-practice contrast.

## Exclusion Criteria

- Reviews used as primary comparators.
- Studies without a verifiable bibliographic identity.
- Duplicate publications or records likely to reuse the same sample.
- Studies whose population or outcome had little relevance to school-age menstrual hygiene.
- Sources for which the claimed result could not be located in the retained evidence.

## Included Set

Twelve studies were retained: five from Pakistan, two from India, two from Nepal, two from Bangladesh, and one contextual Ethiopian study. Eleven have complete local full text. The Yadav et al. Nepal record is limited to its publisher abstract because the journal PDF endpoint returned a server error; only abstract-reported findings are used.

## Notable Exclusions

| Study | Reason for exclusion from the core matrix |
| --- | --- |
| Akbar et al., 2024, rural Lahore | Same location, sample size, age distribution, and collection context as Afzaal et al.; treated as a likely duplicate dataset rather than independent evidence. |
| Mansoori et al., 2020, Pakistan | Large sample but unclear school/district allocation, no parental-education variable, and narrative/table inconsistencies reduced value for the focal comparison. |
| Ali and Rizvi, 2010, Karachi | Historically relevant but superseded by newer Pakistani full-text studies for the core comparison; accessible evidence was limited. |
| Mumtaz et al., 2019, Pakistan | Qualitative study focused on school WASH and social norms; relevant context but not directly comparable to score-based maternal-education analyses. |
| Sahiledengle et al., 2022, Ethiopia | Systematic review; retained in the manuscript's background references but excluded from the primary-study matrix. |

## Screening Limitation

This was a structured, reproducible comparative review rather than a registered systematic review or meta-analysis. Instruments, thresholds, age ranges, and definitions varied too much for pooled estimates. The synthesis therefore compares directions and recurring patterns, not nominal percentages as if they were interchangeable.
